02.1 CASE STUDY — FLAGSHIP

Plannix

An AI-powered lesson planning tool that turns a teacher's standards and objectives into a ready-to-teach K-12 lesson plan in minutes, not hours.

Role
Product Design Lead
Timeline
48-hour hackathon
Team
4 people — design, 2× eng, PM
Tools
Figma, AI research tools

01 PROBLEM

Lesson planning eats the hours teachers don't have.

K-12 teachers routinely spend several hours a week building lesson plans that meet state standards, differentiate for varied skill levels, and still leave room for their own teaching style. Existing tools were either generic worksheet generators or rigid template libraries — neither felt built for the actual planning workflow teachers described in early conversations.

RESEARCH ARTIFACT PLACEHOLDER — swap in interview notes, survey data, or a problem framing diagram

02 PROCESS

Positioning, brand, and prototype — built in parallel.

With 48 hours on the clock, I led product design across three parallel tracks: defining the positioning and value proposition, building a lightweight brand system so the pitch felt like a real product, and prototyping the core planning flow in Figma alongside engineering.

  • Ran quick-turn conversations with teachers to validate the core pain point before designing anything.
  • Defined Plannix's positioning: an assistant that drafts a first-pass lesson plan, not a replacement for a teacher's judgment.
  • Built a name, wordmark, and minimal visual system fast enough to keep pace with engineering.
  • Prototyped the input → generated plan → edit flow in Figma, tested informally with teammates between builds.
  • Structured the final pitch narrative around the problem, the live demo, and what would ship next.
PROCESS ARTIFACT PLACEHOLDER — swap in wireframes, Figma flow screenshots, or brand exploration

03 OUTCOME

A working prototype, pitched live.

Plannix shipped as a working, demoable prototype by the end of the hackathon. The team pitched live to judges, walking through the generated-plan flow end to end, and got direct feedback from teachers on-site that validated the core mechanic: a first-draft plan a teacher would actually want to edit, not discard.
